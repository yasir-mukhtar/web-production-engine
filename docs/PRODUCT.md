# Current Product Definition

> Session status: structure established in Session 0. Content will be drafted after `VISION.md` is active. This draft is not yet authoritative.

## 1. Version and executive definition

Name the current product version and define its bounded promise in plain language.

## 2. Target customer and fit

Define initial customers, qualifying conditions, poor-fit conditions, buyers, users, visitors, and maintainers.

## 3. Jobs and intended outcomes

Define what the founder and client are trying to accomplish and the visitor behavior the website should support.

## 4. End-to-end product experience

Describe founder, client, and maintainer journeys at the outcome level without specifying implementation mechanics.

## 5. Committed capabilities

List version-one commitments grouped by understand, frame, model, create, verify, publish, operate, and learn.

## 6. Supported project envelope

Define supported website types, scale, content, integrations, languages, editorial needs, and delivery model.

## 7. Explicit exclusions and referral conditions

State what version one will not attempt and when work becomes bespoke, deferred, declined, or a different product.

## 8. Roles, responsibilities, and service boundaries

Define founder, client, engine, and third-party responsibilities, including what happens after launch.

## 9. Version-one acceptance scenario

Describe one observable end-to-end scenario that proves the product exists. Leave exact release thresholds to `QUALITY.md`.

## 10. Commitments, defaults, assumptions, and future possibilities

Keep these four categories explicit. Future possibilities are not promises; assumptions require validation.

## 11. Product success measures

Define a small set of outcome measures tied to usefulness, reliability, ownership, founder leverage, and sustainable delivery.

## Drafting acceptance check

Session 2 is complete when a reader can qualify or reject a project, describe exactly what version one promises, distinguish commitments from hypotheses, and avoid deciding how the system is implemented.
